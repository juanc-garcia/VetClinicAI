# Assumptions

## Product Assumptions
- The initial product will serve a single veterinary clinic rather than a multi-location enterprise rollout.
- Staff users will include receptionists, veterinarians, and clinic managers with different access needs.
- Pet records will include basic identity details such as name, species, breed, date of birth, and owner linkage.
- Vaccination, deworming, weight, and medical history data will be tracked as time-based records.
- The owner experience will be delivered through a mobile-friendly web portal rather than a native mobile app in the first release.
- The owner portal will provide a clinic-approved summary of medical information rather than unrestricted raw clinical notes.
- Owners will receive reminders through a configured combination of SMS, email, and push notifications based on clinic and owner preferences.
- The owner experience will include a one-tap emergency contact action that dials a dedicated emergency line.
- The first release focuses on core clinic workflows and owner self-service without complex integrations at launch.

## Business Assumptions
- Clinics want a digital system that reduces manual administrative work and improves follow-up care.
- Pet owners are willing to receive reminders when they are relevant and useful.
- The application will be used by staff who need clear, simple workflows rather than highly technical interfaces.
- Pet owners will value self-service access to their pet's care information and appointments.

## Risks and Open Questions
- What authentication and access-control method will be used for the owner portal?
- What level of medical detail should be visible to owners through the portal?
- Should the emergency contact action be available at all times or only during clinic operating hours?
- What owner consent and privacy preferences should be captured before enabling reminders and portal access?
- How will the clinic define and maintain preventive care schedules such as vaccinations, deworming, and annual exams?
